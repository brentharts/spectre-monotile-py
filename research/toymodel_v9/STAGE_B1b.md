# Stage B1b: class I and III windows from the inflation (in progress, not resolved)

Code: `t9_dissect.py` (dissections by exact cover), `t9_dissect_classes.py`, `t9_dissect_faces.py`, `t9_ifs.py`, `t9_ifs_scan.py`. Data: `dissections.json`, `dissection_choices.json`, `ifs_scan.json`. Labels: **Counted** (search results), **Not resolved**.

## What was found

1. **Group and inflation on the lattice (Exact, asserted in the code).** The 120 icosahedral matrices (generated numerically, entries matched to (a + b tau)/2) act on the 6D coordinates as signed permutations; the inflation matrix S has entries +-1/2 and commutes with them. The lattice generated by the group images of all four tiles' vertices has Smith form (2,2,2,2,2,4) in quarter-units; S maps every tile vertex to a vertex in quarter-units.
2. **Dissections of tau T found by exact cover, not copied from the paper (Counted).** Points reachable from the four vertices of tau T by tile edge vectors inside the tetrahedron (960 to 4560 points), candidate tiles anchored at those points in all 120 orientations (66 to 3898), exact cover of 400 random interior points, volume check. With the tile counts of the inflation matrix (K: 1K+1B; B: 4K+2B+1C; C: 2K+2C+1A; A: 6K+3B+2C) there are **1, 3, 2 and 10** dissections. The targets have no symmetry that fixes their vertex set, so these are genuinely different, 60 combinations in all. A first run of this search also finds dissections of tau A with other tile counts (for example 1B+1C+3A); they are not the Danzer inflation and were discarded. The search finds the paper's counts for all four tiles, which supports the exact cover, but I did not compare the placements with the paper's rotations and translations (its listing has typos).
3. **Face compatibility does not select one (Counted).** Two tiles meeting on a face are dissected independently, so congruent faces must be cut identically. For the 22 congruent face pairs, **none of the 60 combinations is fully compatible**; 8 fail on one pair only, always a face of A against a congruent face of B or C. Either those two faces never meet in the tiling (matching rules not used here), or the correct dissection of tau A is not among those found. Not resolved.
4. **Window attractor machinery (built, inconclusive).** With the parent tile (T, g) at origin x_p, a child (T', g g0) sits at S x_p + R_g p0, and star(S x) = -(1/tau) star(x), so W_T' = union over roles of (-(1/tau) R'_h W_T + c). `t9_ifs.py` tests membership of random points recursively (depth 8 to 9, bounding ball radius 7). The estimated volumes disagree with the frequency ratios (0.65 against 0.55 for B, for example) and the disjointness defect of the pieces is 0.2 to 0.9 for every combination, **also for tile K whose dissection is unique**. The cause is the estimator: the recursion ends by accepting the whole ball, so at depth 8 the set is a neighbourhood of radius about 0.15 around the attractor and its volume is overestimated. These numbers say nothing about the correct dissection and are not used.

## Not resolved, and what to do next

- Estimate the windows from a **patch** instead: apply the chosen dissection n times to one tile (about 4.24^n tiles; n = 8 gives about 10^5), rotate each tile origin's star position back by R'_g^-1, and look at the cloud for each tile type. A correct model-set substitution gives a cloud filling a polytope-like set with uniform density; a wrong one gives a sparse fractal. The test of the whole pipeline is then that one of the vertex windows is the dodecahedron of edge 2 already derived from the Delone cell (STAGE_B1 item 9).
- Decide the dissection of tau A, either by the same cloud test over the 10 candidates, or by reading the paper's Table 3 to compare placements.
- The class I and III windows are still **not** defined. Nothing in this stage changes that.
