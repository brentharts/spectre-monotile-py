# Stages B1 and B3 (partial): ABCK from D6, and the 7-fold inflation factor

Code: `t9_danzer.py --tests-only` (39 of 39 pass; sympy for exact steps, numpy and scipy for the rest). Labels: **Exact** (sympy) or **Numeric** (tolerance 1e-6 or 1e-9).

## B1: what is built and tested

1. **Icosahedral split of R^6 (Exact).** Columns (0,+-1,tau), (+-1,tau,0), (tau,0,+-1) give E_par; the Galois conjugate (tau -> -1/tau) gives E_perp. Rows of each are orthogonal with equal norms (squared norms 5+sqrt5 and 5-sqrt5), E_par E_perp^T = 0, and the two normalised projectors sum to the identity. (A first version of this test omitted the normalisation and failed; the failure was in the test.)
2. **tau-inflation on the lattice (Exact).** The 6x6 matrix S with E_par S = tau E_par and E_perp S = (-1/tau) E_perp satisfies S^2 = S + I, |det S| = 1, and **every entry is +-1/2**. So S does not preserve Z^6 (it moves e_1 outside it) but maps every D6 root e_i + e_j, and random D6 vectors, to D6, and so does S^-1. This is the F-type behaviour: the Danzer tiling lives on D6, not on Z^6. (My first test assumed integer entries and failed; the half-integer entries are the finding.)
3. **The tile K (Numeric).** In the rhombic triacontahedron (12 five-fold vertices, 20 three-fold vertices, 30 rhombic faces) the tetrahedron K = (centre, five-fold vertex, three-fold vertex, face centre) has volume 1/6, and 120 K fill the triacontahedron (volume 20). Its edges include a = sqrt(2+tau)/2 and b = sqrt(3)/2 at scale 1/2, the values quoted in arXiv:2003.13449.
4. **Inflation matrix (Exact, as copied from arXiv:2003.13449).** Perron root tau^3; volumes K : B : C : A = 1 : 2tau : 2tau : 2tau + 2; tile frequencies (K, B, C, A) = 0.51893, 0.28286, 0.16036, 0.03786 (left eigenvector).

5. **Cross-check against Baake and Frettloh's ABCK note** (`ikosa.pdf`, Frettloh's page; read through WebFetch, Table 1, the matrix and the window data). All **Exact** or **Numeric**:
   - Their inflation matrix is the transpose of the one in arXiv:2003.13449, reordered to (A, B, C, K), with eigenvalues tau^3, tau, -1/tau, -tau^-3. Two independent papers agree.
   - Their Table 1 vertex coordinates of A, B, C, K give tetrahedron volumes in the ratio 1 : 2tau : 2tau : 2tau+2 (K : B : C : A), the same as the eigenvector computation; their K has the same edge ratios as our K (1, 1.618, 1.902, 2.618, 2.803, 3.078), at scale tau times ours.
   - The tile frequencies agree with the published A, B, C, K values (0.0379, 0.2829, 0.1604, 0.5189).
   - Vertex densities: with det(D6) fixed in their units by class II (tau/20), the window volumes 20(5tau+2), 4(7tau+4), 20tau^2 give 0.26631, 0.08090 and 0.06910, total 0.41631 (det(D6) = 757.77 in those units).
   - From the note (not tested here): the tiling used is the octahedral version, whose vertices come from the deep hole coset (window a great dodecahedron, class III) and two shallow-hole cosets (a dodecahedron, class II; a star-engraved dodecahedron, class I). D6 vertices themselves do not occur, and the half-integer class IV vertices of ABCK are not produced by this projection.

6. **Table 1 vertices as exact lattice vectors (Exact).** Solving E_par x = (vertex) with E_perp x = its Galois conjugate gives a unique rational x for every vertex of A, B, C and K (our frame and the note's physical coordinates are the same frame). All are half-integer vectors. Relative to the first vertex of the tile, the cosets of D6 in the dual lattice (D6, v = vector class, s and c = the two spinor classes, s by the parity of the coordinate sum) are: A: s, v, s; B: s, v, D6; C: v, v, s; K: v, s and a fourth vertex in none of them (the "class IV" of the note). So the tiles A, B, C use only the three cosets {0, v, s} relative to their first vertex. Since D6 vertices do not occur, that forces the first vertex into the coset c (the other spinor class) and the three vertex classes of the tiling to be the cosets c, v, s. This is deduction from the tile vertices and the note's statement, not a test of the tiling.
7. **A normalisation mismatch (Exact).** The covolume of D6 in our frame is 80 sqrt 5 = 178.885. The note's det(D6), fixed from its class II density, is 757.77, larger by tau^3. Which of the following explains it is **not settled**: the note's window volumes are in different internal units (the windows in our frame would then be smaller), or its densities are per a different volume unit. This matters: a window scaled by the wrong factor gives a wrong vertex set.

8. **The Baake-Grimm window definition (read 2026-11-21: Baake and Grimm, "Mathematical diffraction of aperiodic structures", arXiv:1205.3633, the Danzer section; text only, the window figure is not readable through the fetch).** Lattice: the face-centred module generated by v1+v2, ..., v6-v1, which is our D6 (Exact: these are the roots e_i + e_j). **Star map: algebraic conjugation combined with multiplication by tau**, so their internal coordinates are tau times our E_perp. Three vertex classes, each the points of a coset of the lattice whose star image lies in an icosahedrally symmetric window:
   | class | coset (physical | internal) | window | volume |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | I | deep holes, L + (1,1,1 | tau,tau,tau) | a dodecahedral extension of an icosahedron, pentagonal edge 2 | 20(4 - tau) = 47.64 |
   | II | deep holes, L + (tau,tau,tau | -1,-1,-1) | dodecahedron, edge 2/tau | 4(tau + 2) = 14.47 |
   | III | shallow holes, L + (tau,0,1 | -1,0,tau) | great dodecahedron, pentagonal edge 2 | 20(tau - 1) = 12.36 |
   - **Comparison with Frettloh's note (Exact arithmetic):** his volumes 20(5tau+2), 4(7tau+4), 20 tau^2 (201.8, 61.3, 52.4) are exactly tau^3 times these (ratio 4.236068 for all three), and the class proportions agree (0.64, 0.19, 0.17). **Correction (2026-11-21, item 9):** I first read this as a disagreement about the vertex density. It is not: a window volume and the covolume of the lattice both scale by tau^3 when the internal coordinates are multiplied by tau, so the density is the same in both frames (class II: 4(tau+2)/(80 sqrt5) = 4(7tau+4)/757.77 = tau/20 = 0.0809). The "0.098 total and 6.7 tiles per vertex" that I wrote here came from dividing the already rescaled volumes by 178.9 again, and is withdrawn.
   - **The two sources also label the hole types differently** (BG: I and II deep, III shallow; the note: III deep, I and II shallow), and the note's class I is a "dodecahedron with stars engraved" where BG says "dodecahedral extension of an icosahedron". I have not resolved this.
   - **The window orientation is fixed by symmetry (Exact reasoning, not coded):** the internal action of the icosahedral group is the Galois conjugate of its physical action, so a window must be invariant under the conjugate group, whose five-fold axes are along (0, tau, +-1) in star coordinates (the star image tau conj(0,1,tau) = (0,tau,-1)). That fixes the orientation of the dodecahedron (face normals along those axes) and of the great dodecahedron (vertices along them).
   - **Still not defined: the two star-shaped solids as point sets** (the great dodecahedron as a solid and the dodecahedral extension of the icosahedron). A star polyhedron has no unique interior; I computed 12 pentagonal pyramids over its pentagons, 23.4 at edge 2, which matches neither volume. So the solids cannot be written down from these texts.

9. **Windows from the Delone cells of D6 (Exact/Numeric, route (a), 6 tests).** Following the dual description in Zeidler and Kramer (arXiv:math-ph/9909012: the window is a projected Voronoi cell, its vertices are of hole types a and c; tiles correspond to Delaunay cells; no ABCK in that paper, no explicit hole coordinates), I projected the Delone cells of D6 to the star frame (tau times conjugation):
   - the shallow-hole cell, a 6-cross-polytope (12 vertices +-e_j around e_1), projects to an **icosahedron of edge 2**, volume 17.4536;
   - the deep-hole cell with odd weight (half-cube with 32 vertices, centre (1/2,...,1/2)) projects to a **regular dodecahedron of edge 2**, volume 61.305 = 4(7tau+4), exactly the note's class II window, with covolume 757.77 and density tau/20 = 0.0809. So the note's class II is the plain projected Delone cell, in the note's scale;
   - the even-weight half-cube projects to a larger icosahedron (12 hull vertices, volume 73.93), which is **not** the class I window 20(5tau+2) = 201.8;
   - the class III volume 20 tau^2 is three times the icosahedron volume above, so the great dodecahedron window is not the projected cell (the icosahedron) either, but is related to it by that factor 3.
   So one of the three windows is now derived (class II), and the density arithmetic is consistent across both sources. Classes I and III are not plain Delone-cell projections; they need the rule that the tiling actually uses for holes, which neither text states.
   - **Open puzzle:** with the note's total vertex density 0.4163 and the Table 1 tile density 0.658 per unit volume, tiles per vertex is 1.58. The note's tiling hABCKi is the octahedral version, and ABCK has further (class IV) vertices, so the ratio to the Table 1 tetrahedra is not comparable; I have not resolved this and it should not be used as evidence either way.
