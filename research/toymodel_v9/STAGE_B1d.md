# Stage B1d: explicit windows of the three Danzer D6 vertex classes

Input: the star-frame clouds (`t9_win_cloud.py 11` -> `win_cloud_L11.npz`, 3 min, 5 MB) of the dissection selected in STAGE_B1c. Scripts: `t9_win_radial.py` (radial function, class identification), `t9_win_sym.py` (symmetry test), `t9_win_fold.py` (fold into the fundamental cone, x120 statistics), `t9_win_planes.py` (RANSAC plane fit), `t9_win_def.py` (explicit definitions), `t9_win_oracle.py` (tabulated oracle for class I), `t9_win_tests.py` (6 tests, all pass).

## Results (Numeric, Counted unless stated)
1. **Symmetry.** All three clouds are invariant under one icosahedral group, B, with five-fold axes (0,tau,1) and cyclic permutations (voxel IoU >= 0.94; the other chirality, axes (0,1,tau), gives 0.57 to 0.81). The three-fold axes of B are (1,1,1), (0,1/tau,tau) and cyclic, i.e. the vertices of the dodecahedron with faces on the B five-fold axes. (Earlier in this stage I mixed up the two chiralities, which cost an hour; the fix is in `t9_win_def.py`, which builds the axes from the B group.)
2. **Class II** (volume 4(tau+2)): the convex dodecahedron of edge 2/tau, faces on the five-fold axes, inradius 1.376 (fit 1.359, sampling bias). Explicit: `inII`. Volume 14.453 vs 14.472; 99.87% of the cloud inside.
3. **Class III** (volume 20(tau-1)): the **great dodecahedron**. Its 12 vertices are the vertices of the icosahedron of edge 2 (on the five-fold axes, circumradius sqrt(tau sqrt5) = 1.902); its faces are the 12 pentagons of the planes n_k.x = tau/rho (n_k unit five-fold axis, rho = 1.902, h = 0.8507). A single plane family fits the whole boundary (RANSAC: 1 plane, 2556 of 2556 surface points). Explicit: icosahedron of edge 2 minus the 20 pits {x : n_k.x > tau/rho for the 3 five-fold axes k nearest to the three-fold axis m}. Volume 12.365 vs 12.361; 99.77% of the cloud inside; no empty volume.
4. **Class I** (volume 20(4-tau)): bounded by the dodecahedron of edge 2 (faces on the five-fold axes, h = 2.209 fit vs 2.227 exact; vertices on the three-fold axes at 2.80), with 12 deep pits, one over each face. Each pit has its apex at 1.14 to 1.18 on the five-fold axis (1.176 is the circumradius of the icosahedron of edge 2/tau) and is bounded by planes of the form n_k.x = 0.51 to 0.53 (1/rho = 0.5257) with n_k the five neighbouring five-fold axes, but the pit is much wider than the intersection of those five half-spaces: the cross-section grows to a five-pointed star, tips toward the face vertices, ratio of tip to waist about 2.3. The pit volume needed is 48 tau - 64 = 13.665 total (1.14 per pit).
5. **No exact formula for class I yet.** The 5-plane cone (all five n_k.x > 1/rho) removes only 4.2 of the 13.7 volume (window volume 57.1 against 47.64), so it is Negative as a definition. The RANSAC on the pit boundary gave extra planes with n at 31.7 degrees from the axis (two-fold directions) but at inconsistent offsets (h = 1.14 to 1.37), so I have not pinned them. What exists is a tabulated radial oracle (`RadialOracle`, nearest cell on the fundamental cone, 200x200 grid): volume 47.26 to 47.44 vs 47.64 (0.4 to 0.8%), 99.7% of the cloud inside, no accepted cell empty of cloud. The window is star-shaped about the origin (radial-function volume 47.8 vs 47.64).

## What this gives B2
- Classes II and III: exact membership. Class I: membership accurate at about 1% of volume on a shell of width about 0.05 around the surface (error band to be quantified before it enters a flip count).
- The Danzer vertex set is now a model set up to that error: vertices = Z^6 points in D6 cosets of the three classes whose star image lies in the class window (plus the class IV quarter-lattice vertices, window volume about 1.0, not characterised).
- Open: class IV window; exact class I pit; the Danzer scale (our windows are in the "star frame" where the class I dodecahedron has edge 2; Baake-Grimm volumes agree, Frettloh's are tau^3 larger); the vertex-density puzzle against 0.4163.
- Nothing from track B is in `quantgrav.bib`; no sources were read in this stage; the great-dodecahedron identification is from the data and agrees with the Baake-Grimm window list from STAGE_B1 (III: great dodecahedron).
