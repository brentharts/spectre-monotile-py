\title{Seam Automata, Phason Flips, and Dipolar Pair Qubits on the Spectre Monotile}

\author{Brent Hartshorn \orcidlink{0009-0004-2853-655X}, Tristan Romanov and James Lockwood}
\date{\today}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent This paper combines exact combinatorics, flip dynamics and quantum many-body calculations on the Spectre aperiodic monotile, and applies a few quantum-gravity diagnostics to them. In the Spectre a tiling is a point of an internal space, and moving that point by a small step rearranges the tiles (a phason flip). We ask what such a flip does to the tiling and to its connectivity, and what Hamiltonian governs the two-valued degree of freedom, the position of the $14$th vertex, that the tile carries. The tiles that can carry this bit come in bump-and-dent pairs; we prove that their number obeys a linear recurrence of order five at every level of the substitution, show that the local rule placing them on supertile boundaries is a finite table of $53$ types that closes at level $4$, and show that a hole of up to $300$ tiles in a level-$4$ patch has exactly one refilling. The area ledger reduces each pair to a single qubit with frustrated dipolar couplings, whose classical ground state is unique for up to $28$ pairs. A flip is a cluster of tiles at uniform density, and in three quarters of the flips that change the tiles around a footprint the contact graph of those tiles is not isomorphic before and after, while the tetrad flux of the lifted tiling stays zero. The flip relation is symmetric under reversal, so it gives no arrow of time. We could not decide whether the flip clusters are tilings, and hence whether a flip is a Pachner-type move. The largest pairwise concurrence of the pair qubits of exact patches peaks in a window of the ratio of tunnelling to coupling that the Spectre--Hat flow crosses, but it does not grow with the number of pairs ($0.65$ at ten, $0.35$ at sixteen). The relative entropy of a region of the ground state follows its volume, not its boundary, and no area observable of the model is proportional to it; against a thermal reference the local first law $S_{\mathrm{rel}}=\beta\,\Delta\langle H_A\rangle$ holds for a region only when the state is hot, and in the ground state the modular Hamiltonian of a region below about $40\%$ of the system has a local inverse temperature that grows with depth and reverses above half. A path-integral Monte Carlo of the stoquastic pair model reproduces exact diagonalisation to $16$ pairs and does not converge at $82$ and $804$. The quantum-gravity tests (Pachner moves, tetrad flux, an area--relative-entropy relation, modular Hamiltonians) mostly return negative or limited answers; the positive results are the exact structure of the pairs and the behaviour of the pair-qubit model.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
Discrete approaches to quantum gravity ask for a background whose elementary changes are local, reversible and rich enough to carry geometry: moves on a triangulation \cite{pachner1991}, rewrites of a graph. Aperiodic tilings have a ready-made family of such changes, which makes them a natural place to apply the diagnostics those approaches use. We do so as a test, not as a proposal of a theory of gravity. A tiling of the plane by the Spectre \cite{smith2023}, the strictly chiral aperiodic monotile, is a model set: a tile is present if-and-only-if (iff) the internal image of its lattice vector lies in a fractal window \cite{bgms}. Moving the internal phase by a small step $\delta$ (a \emph{phason step} \cite{socolar1986}) removes tiles near the window boundary and adds others elsewhere. We call the ordered pair of phases a \emph{flip}. The Spectre also carries a natural two-valued degree of freedom, the position of its $14$th vertex, which becomes a qubit once the tiling's area ledger is imposed.

\emph{Research question and scope.} What does a flip do to the tiling and to its connectivity, and what is the Hamiltonian of the qubits it carries? The first question is answered by exact enumeration and measurement on patches, the second by exact reductions and numerical solution of a pair-qubit model. The paper is organised around these two parts: exact combinatorics and flip dynamics of an aperiodic tiling, and quantum information and statistical mechanics of the qubits it carries. We then ask which quantum-gravity diagnostics those parts pass or fail: locality of the move set, an arrow of time, a connectivity change, a relation between an area observable and a relative entropy, a local modular Hamiltonian. Most of these fail or are undecided, and we report them as such.

The whole paper is one pipeline, read from the top: a substrate fixes what a flip is, the tile's bit gives a Hamiltonian, the Hamiltonian's states are tested against relative entropy and a Hopf structure, and what is left over is stated as hypotheses.

\section{The substrate and the bit}\label{sec:model}
\subsection{The Spectre, its lift and its windows}
The Spectre has $14$ edges of unit length at multiples of $30^\circ$ \cite{smith2023}; the Hat \cite{smith2024hat} belongs to the same two-parameter family. In the lift of Ch\'eritat and Ma \cite{cheritat2023} a vertex is an integer vector $(p,q,r,s)$ with plane position $u+\zeta v$, $u=p+q\varpi$, $v=r+s\varpi$, $\varpi=e^{i\pi/3}$, $\zeta=e^{i\pi/6}$: the lift lattice is $\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_{12}]$, a red $A_2$ (edges at even multiples of $30^\circ$) plus a green $A_2$. All exact computations below use these integer vectors. (The lift lattice embeds isometrically in $E_8$ with every edge a root, but the inflation does not extend to $E_8$; we do not use $E_8$ here.)

The vertices form a model set: a tile $(b,u{+}w)$ of class $b$ is present at internal phase $z$ iff $B_bz+d_{b,w}\in Z_0(S_b)$, where $Z_0$ is a window with fractal boundary.
\begin{result}[\exact, source]\label{res:window-pieces}
The window of a tile of shape $O$ (the tile) or $G$ (the two-tile ``Gamma'' shape) in the frame of parity $p\in\{0,1\}$ is an attractor of a graph-directed system of similarities of ratio $1/\lambda$, $\lambda^2=4+\sqrt{15}$ ($\lambda=\qgKLam$). The pieces $Z_0(O)$, $Z_1(O)$, $Z_0(G)$, $Z_1(G)$ have $\qgKPieces$ children, and the seams between the children have box dimension $\log(5+2\sqrt6)/\log(4+\sqrt{15})=\qgKSeam$ (a lower bound for the boundary dimension of the window). We rasterise these maps and do not re-prove them.
\end{result}
\begin{result}[\exact\snap]\label{res:index36}
The tile vectors of every window class of the level-$3$ patch lie in a coset of one sublattice of $\mathbb{Z}^4$ of index $\qgKIndex$ (Smith normal form $1,1,6,6$; $\qgKTilevec$ tile vectors, $\qgKClasses$ classes). A placement $(b,w)$ is possible only if $w\equiv v_b-u_a$ modulo that sublattice.
\end{result}
Without this restriction a first attempt put $36$ to $39$ windows over an interior point instead of one. The windows of neighbouring tiles cover internal space with no hole and no detected overlap at the resolution tested, but they do not have the Wada property (three or more pieces meeting at every boundary point): at a tenth of the boundary pixels three or more pieces lie within the radius $0.064$, and the children of one piece are not a Wada partition ($\dagger$, measured on rasterised windows; see Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).

\subsection{Flips}
A \emph{phason step} replaces $z$ by $z'=z+\delta e$, $|e|=1$. A \emph{flip} is the ordered pair $(z,z')$ when the set of tiles present, $\mathcal{T}(z)$, differs from $\mathcal{T}(z')$, and its content is $\mathcal{F}(z,z')=\mathcal{T}(z)\triangle\mathcal{T}(z')$. Since $\mathcal{F}(z',z)=\mathcal{F}(z,z')$, a flip has no direction by construction.

\subsection{The bit and the pair qubit}\label{sec:bit}
Of the $14$ vertices the eighth, $P_8$, is flat in the straight outline. In the model it carries a bit $s=\pm1$: with $s=+1$ it stands out of the chord $P_7P_9$ by $h$ (a convex tile), with $s=-1$ it stands in by $h$ (a reflex tile). On a tiling of the plane a tile can open only together with a partner: two tiles whose eighth vertices meet alone, a \emph{bump-and-dent pair} (Section~\ref{sec:pairs}). The \emph{area ledger} $Q=\sigma^z_i+\sigma^z_j=0$ then holds for the pair, which is the constraint that turns the two bits into one qubit (Section~\ref{sec:ham}).

\section{Exact combinatorics of the pairs}\label{sec:pairs}
Let $T_k$ be the number of tiles of the level-$k$ patch (built by $k$ substitutions; $1,9,71,559,4401,34649$ tiles at levels $0$ to $5$, $T_k=8T_{k-1}-T_{k-2}$) and $P_k$ its number of bump-and-dent pairs. At levels $2,3,4$ the patches have $\qgWPatchTilesTwo$, $\qgWPatchTilesThree$, $\qgWPatchTilesFour$ tiles and $\qgWPairsTwo$, $\qgWPairsThree$, $\qgWPairsFour$ pairs.
\begin{result}[\exact]\label{res:found-pairs}
$T_k=A\mu^k+B\mu^{-k}$ with $\mu=4+\sqrt{15}$, and the counts $P_k=0,0,6,82,804,7012,58106,\qgWPairSeven$ for $k=0,\dots,7$ satisfy a recurrence with characteristic polynomial $(x-1)(x^2-8x+1)(x^2-4x-1)$: the roots are those of the tile counts ($\mu^{\pm1}$) and of the perimeter law of the supertiles ($\nu=2+\sqrt5=\varphi^3$, $2-\sqrt5$, and $1$). The recurrence holds for every $k$ (Result~\ref{res:found-pairs-proof}). From it
\[
P_k=\frac{\mu^k-\mu^{-k}}{\sqrt{15}}-\Bigl(\tfrac14+\tfrac{3\sqrt5}{20}\Bigr)\nu^k-\Bigl(\tfrac14-\tfrac{3\sqrt5}{20}\Bigr)(2-\sqrt5)^k+\tfrac12,
\]
so $P_k/T_k\to2/(5+\sqrt{15})=(5-\sqrt{15})/5=\rho_0=\qgRhoZero$, the density of valence-$4$ vertices, with a correction of order $(\nu/\mu)^k=\qgWBoundaryRatio^k$. At level $3$, $\qgWPairPctThree\%$ of the tiles are in a pair.
\end{result}
\begin{result}[\exact]\label{res:found-pairs-proof}
The recurrence of Result~\ref{res:found-pairs} holds for every $k$. Call $O$ any of the eight species other than $\Gamma$, and $G$ the species $\Gamma$. By induction on $k$ the eight species $O$ have the same tile set, outline and flat-pair count $P_k(O)$, because their substitution rules differ only in the species of the children; $\Gamma$ omits the child in slot $2$ and has $P_k(G)$. Write the seam of two adjacent children as a relative transform in $\mathbb{Z}[\omega]^4$, $\omega=e^{i\pi/3}$. The reflection $R=-\overline{(\cdot)}$ cancels in every transform between children of the same level, and the reachable seams close on $15$ states ($13$ occur in $O$, $11$ in $G$) with an integer transition matrix $M\in\mathbb{Z}^{15\times15}$. No triple junction creates a flat pair, so $P_{k+1}(O)=7P_k(O)+P_k(G)+J_{k+1}(O)$ and $P_{k+1}(G)=6P_k(O)+P_k(G)+J_{k+1}(G)$, where $J_k$ counts the pairs across seams and is linear in $M^k\mathbf b_1$. The Krylov space of $\mathbf b_1$ under $M$ has dimension $4$ (eigenvalues $2\pm\sqrt5$, $+1$, $-1$), and the $(-1)^k$ mode vanishes in the read-out of both $O$ and $G$, so $J_k$ has minimal polynomial $(x-1)(x^2-4x-1)$ for $k\ge2$, and $P_k$ the polynomial above for all $k\ge0$ ($O$) and $k\ge1$ ($G$).
\end{result}
The computation uses exact integer and rational arithmetic (closure of the automaton, $M\mathbf b_k=\mathbf b_{k+1}$ for $k\le3$, the Krylov dimension and the counts to level $7$; code in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}). The step from these checks to all $k$ is the closure of the automaton; no second, independent program has confirmed it.

\subsection{The local pair rule}\label{sec:rule}
A level-$L$ supertile of one tile of the level-$(L{-}1)$ patch is a block; no flat pair lies inside a block ($0$ of $6$, $82$, $804$ at levels $2,3,4$), every pair of level $L-1$ reappears as one pair of level $L$, and the others lie on boundaries between adjacent blocks. Does the number of pairs on a boundary depend only on a finite label of the two blocks and the edges they share?
\begin{result}[\exact\snap\ for levels $2$ to $6$]\label{res:ham-rg-closed}
Give each tile its leaf label, the species $c$ of the tile it was created as (a tile of species $c$ is inflated into the supertile of species $c$), and its kind (single, or one of the two halves of a Mystic pair). The type of the seam between two adjacent blocks is the two labels with the shared edge pairs, taken up to exchange of the blocks; its count is the number of flat pairs on the seam. At levels $2$ to $6$ ($6$, $82$, $804$, $7012$, $58106$ pairs) no pair lies inside a block or joins blocks that are not adjacent, a seam carries at most one pair, and the count is a function of the type. The numbers of types are $14$, $46$, $53$, $53$, $53$ ($6$, $28$, $35$, $35$, $35$ of them with a pair), and the sets at levels $4$, $5$, $6$ are equal: no new type appears after level $4$. Keyed instead by the outermost species of the tile (a position in the whole hierarchy, not a property of the block) the table does not close ($\qgZTypesFour$ types at level $4$, $\qgZTypesNew$ of them new).
\end{result}
\hypo: no new type appears at any level (a proof would need the seam automaton of Result~\ref{res:found-pairs-proof} to determine the type). The table was rebuilt from a previously reported one, which has $66$ types when blocks are ordered by index and $53$ up to exchange; code in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}.

\subsection{Orientation parity}
With the orientation index $m=\text{angle}/30^\circ\bmod12$, the minority parity $m\bmod2$ is exactly the Mystic-shaped tiles.
\begin{result}[\exact\snap]\label{res:flip-parity}
The minority parity counts $1,8,63,496,3905$ at levels $1$ to $5$; the count at one level is the majority count at the previous one, and the minority frequency tends to $(5-\sqrt{15})/10=0.11270$ with ratio majority to minority $4+\sqrt{15}=\lambda^2$. Hence the window of a Mystic pair has $1/(3+\sqrt{15})=0.14550$ of the area of a single tile's window; \numeric: measured $0.1578$, $0.1515$, $0.1479$, $0.1460$ at tolerances $0.012$, $0.006$, $0.003$, $0.0015$. The orientation frequencies are window areas.
\end{result}

\section{Phason flips}\label{sec:flips}
We follow the tile of class $0$ at the origin of a level-$3$ patch. All numbers marked $\dagger$ are for one class, one footprint and a level-$3$ patch, so none is a statement about all tilings.

\subsection{The engine}\label{sec:flips-engine}
The engine lists the placements $(b,w)$ in the coset of Result~\ref{res:index36} inside a lattice box and tests membership in a window by the nearest point of a depth-$7$ cloud: inside if nearer than $\mathrm{IN}_T$, outside if farther than $\mathrm{OUT}_T$, otherwise the phase is dropped.
\begin{result}[\numeric\snap]\label{res:flip-engine}
(i) \emph{Box.} The box must contain the Mystic-pair placements, whose footprints are displaced from the physical part of $w$ (class $5$, $|w_{\rm phys}|=12.77$). With a box of radius $20$ every one of $1934$ real neighbour pairs around class-$0$ tiles of real level-$3$ patches is in the list, all in the same coset; a box of $12$ loses $5$ of the pairs and leaves $12\%$ of the phases of $Z_0(O)$ uncovered. (ii) \emph{Tolerance.} A flip occurs only where $z$ is within $\delta$ of a window boundary, so if $\mathrm{IN}_T$ is comparable to $\delta$ both the old and the new tile count as present, exactly in the changed samples: $\mathrm{IN}_T=0.003$, $\delta=0.01$ gives overlapping tiles in $37$ of $60$ changed samples against $0$ of $60$ unchanged; $\mathrm{IN}_T=0.0015$ (cloud spacing $0.0012$), $\delta=0.03$ gives $5$ and $1$ of $60$, and $1180$ of $1321$ changed samples ($89\%$) have a valid tiling before and after.
\end{result}
All results below use box radius $20$, $\mathrm{IN}_T=0.0015$, $\mathrm{OUT}_T=0.0035$, $\delta=0.03$.

\subsection{A flip is a cluster at uniform density}\label{sec:flips-clusters}
\begin{result}[\numeric\snap]\label{res:flip-clusters}
One phase changes about $10$ tiles in a region of radius $14$ about the origin tile (mean $9.9$ removed, median $11$, at most $37$), uniformly in distance: the frequency of change per placement is $0.0021$, $0.0020$, $0.0022$, $0.0022$, $0.0023$ in the shells $0$--$3$, $3$--$6$, $6$--$9$, $9$--$12$, $12$--$14$. Linking removed and added tiles that overlap gives $818$ clusters; the first twelve inspected have $8$ to $17$ tiles on each side and areas $80$ to $172$ (Figure~\ref{fig:flip-gallery}). The tile count differs by one where a Mystic pair is exchanged for two single tiles. No cluster satisfies the closure test, because the region in which the placement list is complete (radius $8.5$) is smaller than the clusters.
\end{result}
\begin{result}[\numeric\snap]\label{res:flip-density}
The mean number of changed tiles inside a disc of radius $\rho$ about the tile grows as $\rho^{2.25}$ ($\rho=1.5$ to $7$, $2831$ usable phases), and the number of changed tiles per tile present is constant, $0.056$ at every $\rho$ (a lower bound, since phases inside the ambiguity band are dropped).
\end{result}
The growth exponent does not depend on the step ($2.19$, $2.25$, $2.33$ at $\delta=0.06$, $0.03$, $0.015$; the step $0.0075$ is below the resolution of the tolerance), and the elementary flip is a cluster of about two tiles at the origin that does not shrink to a single swap as the step halves. So a step changes tiles with a positive density over the whole plane: a pattern of local clusters, not an isolated event. The same picture holds for the phason displacement of the internal point itself:
\begin{result}[\numeric\snap]\label{res:phason}
For a Gaussian displacement of standard deviation $\delta$ of the internal point of $Z_0(O)$, the probability of leaving the window follows $\delta^{\alpha}$ with $\alpha=\qgKAexit$ for $\delta=0.012$ to $0.048$, the value $2-D=\qgKAexpect$ expected from $D=\qgKSeam$. In the variable $T=\delta^2$ of a thermal noise the slope is $\qgKTexpect$.
\end{result}
A thermal phason noise of variance $T$ should then change a fraction $\propto T^{\alpha/2}$ of the tiles; this is a prediction for a Langevin simulation on internal space that was not run (\hypo).

\subsection{The contact graph changes; the field sheets do not}\label{sec:flips-contact}
The footprint is the origin tile enlarged by a margin of $1$ ($767$ placements); the graph has the tiles meeting the footprint as vertices and contact along an edge as links.
\begin{result}[\numeric\snap]\label{res:flip-contact}
Of $6000$ phases ($5245$ unambiguous) $539$ change the present set, and $468$ of these have a valid tiling before and after. In $224$ ($48\%$) the set of tiles meeting the footprint changes, and in $169$ of those ($75\%$) the contact graph is not isomorphic before and after; in $55$ it is isomorphic as an unlabelled graph and differs in the classes. The reversed step gives identical counts.
\end{result}

\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{research/toymodel_v8/figures/fig_rerun_contact.png}
\caption{Result~\ref{res:flip-contact}. (a) Outcomes of the $468$ valid pairs; solid bars forward, hatched bars the reversed step. (b) Number of tiles meeting the footprint before and after.}\label{fig:flip-contact}
\end{figure}
\begin{result}[\exact; \hypo\ for the identification]\label{res:lift-flux}
Identify the four components of a tetrad index with the four coordinates of the lift lattice $\mathbb{Z}^4$ (this identification is ours). The edge vectors of any tiling that is a lift are differences of lattice points, so the one-forms $A^{\hat\alpha}$ are exact and the field strength $F^{\hat\alpha}=dA^{\hat\alpha}$ is zero on every closed loop of the tiling graph, for every phase. A phason flip, which changes which tiles touch, leaves every flux equal to zero: every direction is annihilated by $F$, the field sheets are trivial and there is nothing to reconnect.
\end{result}
The two notions of connectivity therefore disagree on the same flips. This is a precise statement about this model; it does not make a flip a reconnection in the sense of Comisso and Asenjo \cite{comisso2026} (Section~\ref{sec:reconnect}). \hypo: that the contact graph is the right discrete stand-in for the connectivity of the sheets.

\subsection{No arrow of time from flips}
\begin{result}[\exact; \negres]\label{res:flip-symmetry}
The present set at a phase is a function of the phase, and ``the region is tiled without overlap or hole'' and ``the two contact graphs are isomorphic'' are symmetric in the two phases. So $(z,z')$ is a valid flip iff $(z',z)$ is, and every count of flips by type is identical in the two orientations. \numeric\snap: the reversed run of Result~\ref{res:flip-contact} reproduces all counts.
\end{result}
An arrow would need an initial condition, a rate violating detailed balance between local patterns, or the direction of the inflation (which contracts internal space by $1/\lambda$ and changes the relative size of a step by $\lambda$ per level); none was found, and the last has not been tested.

\subsection{Parity and erasure}
\begin{result}[\numeric\snap]\label{res:flip-parity-change}
The local parity is not conserved by flips. Among events inside a disc in which the area $n_O+2n_G$ is unchanged, the number of Mystic pairs $n_G$ changed in $0.32$ of $163$ events at $\rho=7$; the fraction is not smooth in $\rho$, so only its sign is stable. A pair exchanged for two singles changes $n_G$ by $-1$ and $n_O$ by $+2$ at fixed area.
\end{result}
\begin{result}[\negres\snap]\label{res:flip-erasure}
Erase the origin tile, keep the tiles outside it, draw $50$ phases within $0.05$ of the true one and keep those whose outside is unchanged: in no case is the set of tiles meeting the erased tile ambiguous ($200$ phases drawn, $184$ used). Places where three or more window pieces meet are not where erasure is harder. One tile, exterior radius $7$.
\end{result}

\subsection{Exact refilling of a hole}\label{sec:flips-cavity}
The point cloud cannot say whether a cluster of changed tiles is a tiling. We ask exactly on the level-$4$ patch ($4401$ tiles of one chirality, vertices as integer vectors): erase $k$ tiles and list every filling of the hole by Spectres, edge to edge and without overlap. A search takes an open vertex of the hole (its tiles cover less than $360^\circ$), lists the tiles that fit its free directions without overlapping anything (an exact test over $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt3)$), and continues at the vertex with the fewest candidates, so a single candidate is a forced move. It is complete for edge-to-edge tilings, which holds on the central tiles tested, and since all tiles have equal area a filling by $k$ tiles covers the hole. No SAT solver is used.
\begin{result}[\exact\snap\ for the holes tried]\label{res:flip-cavity}
For the $k$ tiles nearest to a central tile, $k=1$ (all twelve orientations), $10$, $20$, $40$, $80$, $150$ and $300$ (radius $27.96$), the only filling is the original. Every step is forced: the search visits $k+1$ nodes for $k\le40$ and $88$, $170$, $371$ nodes for $k=80$, $150$, $300$ ($8$ minutes at $k=300$). So no flip of the tiles of such a hole exists that leaves the rest of the patch fixed.
\end{result}
This is the exact form of Result~\ref{res:flip-erasure}, at $300$ tiles instead of one. It does not say that the clusters of Result~\ref{res:flip-clusters} are absent: they may reach outside the hole, or be artefacts of the cloud tolerance; to decide this one must refill the holes of the flips that are valid tilings. The holes are one family (nearest $k$, one centre, one class). An independent SAT computation (CaDiCaL, certificates checked with \texttt{drat-trim}) reports the same uniqueness to $300$ tiles, and in addition a $2$-tile swap at $k=4$ when reflected tiles are allowed. We do not reproduce that swap: with reflections allowed we find no second filling in $57$ small holes, and the holes of the reported swap were not available to us.

\subsection{What the flips say about physics}\label{sec:flips-physics}
\emph{Moves.} In two dimensions two triangulations of one closed surface are related by a finite sequence of bistellar moves \cite{pachner1991}, so ``a flip is a composite of local moves'' is close to automatic; what would carry information is the length of the word and the size of the cluster, and for quantum gravity the four-dimensional moves, for which a planar tiling is only a slice of the lift in $\mathbb{Z}^4$. The clusters inspected have $8$ to $17$ tiles per side, so a flip is not a single elementary move; no closed cluster was reached and nothing here supports or contradicts a link to quantum gravity.
\emph{Time.} The orientation is not in the flips (Result~\ref{res:flip-symmetry}). A candidate for a third source is the inflation: windows of consecutive levels differ in size by $1/\lambda$, so a flip rate that depends on the level would give a direction (\hypo; not tested).
\emph{Codes.} The absence of ambiguous erasure (Results~\ref{res:flip-erasure}, \ref{res:flip-cavity}) and the positive density of change in every disc are what a locally recoverable pattern needs; neither is a quantum code, and erasure was a classical rule. A Landauer-type cost \cite{landauer1961} would need a record of the erased tile, which was not modelled.

\section{A Hamiltonian for the bits}\label{sec:ham}
Every term in this section is a definition. Any state on a tensor product, read out locally, has CHSH at most $2\sqrt2$ \cite{cirelson1980}, so a Hamiltonian cannot be credited with the quantum bound, and a Hamiltonian of local constraints is a quantum constraint satisfaction problem whose ground state solves the classical one when the terms commute \cite{gharibian2015}.

\subsection{The pair qubit}\label{sec:ham-pair}
Put a qubit on each tile, $\sigma^z=+1$ convex and $-1$ reflex. With the area ledger as a penalty,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hbit}
H=-J\sum_{\mathrm{pairs}}\sigma^x_i\sigma^x_j+g\sum_{\mathrm{pairs}}(\sigma^z_i+\sigma^z_j)^2-\sum_{A<B}K_{AB}\,\sigma^z_{A_1}\sigma^z_{B_1}-\varepsilon\sum_i\sigma^z_i .
\end{equation}
\begin{result}[\exact]\label{res:ham-pair}
$\sigma^x_i\sigma^x_j$ flips both tiles of a pair, so it commutes with $Q^2$: the sector $Q=0$ is invariant for every $g$, and in it each pair is one qubit $\tau$ with $H_{\mathrm{eff}}=-J\sum\tau^x-\sum K_{AB}\tau^z_A\tau^z_B$; no limit $g\to\infty$ is needed. The uniform field vanishes on the sector, so the area term cannot bias a pair. One pair cannot violate CHSH: the only one-tile observables that commute with the ledger are functions of $\sigma^z$. For two pairs the ground state has concurrence $C=K/\sqrt{4J^2+K^2}$ \cite{wootters1998} and largest CHSH value $2\sqrt{1+C^2}$ \cite{horodecki1995}.
\end{result}

\subsection{Frustrated dipoles on patch clusters}\label{sec:ham-patch}
On a patch the pairs are disjoint and the same along the flow, so each carries a pair qubit. The coupling is not chosen: with the bit read as a charge $\pm h$ at the centroid of its tile, a pair is a dipole and $K_{AB}$ is the Coulomb energy of two dipoles.
\begin{result}[\exact; \negres\ for the two-pair picture]\label{res:ham-patch}
The charges give an exact Ising coupling with both signs ($\qgYAntiFour$ of the couplings antiferromagnetic at level $4$), falling as $r^{-3}$ (fitted slope $\qgYSlope$). The patch is frustrated: the classical ground state leaves $\qgYFrusTwo$ of the bond weight unsatisfied on the six pairs of level $2$, and $\qgYFrusLo$ to $\qgYFrusHi$ on the central $6$ to $14$ pairs of level $3$. As the tunnelling goes to zero, the ground state of every such cluster is the cat $(|s\rangle+|{-s}\rangle)/\sqrt2$ of its classical ground state $s$, whose two-pair reductions have concurrence below $10^{-10}$. For two pairs that cat is a Bell state, which is why the two-pair model is most entangled at the Spectre end.
\end{result}
\begin{result}[\exact\snap; \numeric\snap]\label{res:ham-dipole-large}
Result~\ref{res:ham-patch} continues beyond $14$ pairs. The exact ground state of the $N$ central pairs of level $3$ (all $2^{N-1}$ states, split in two blocks, Python with \texttt{numpy}) is unique up to the global flip for every even $N$ from $6$ to $28$, with unsatisfied weight $f=0.1649$ at $N=14$ and $0.1695$, $0.1723$, $0.1937$, $0.2046$, $0.2062$, $0.2111$, $0.2091$ at $N=16,\dots,28$; $f$ is not monotone in $N$. Annealing with descent agrees with the exact value at $N=14$ and $20$. Beyond that it gives upper bounds only, $f\le0.2293$ for the whole level-$3$ patch ($N=82$) and $f\le0.294$ for level $4$ ($N=804$), and the best energy is reached by one restart of $32$ to $128$, so these are not converged. \hypo: that $f$ tends to a bulk value near $0.29$.
\end{result}
The exact values for $N=16$ to $22$ of an independent compiled solver are reproduced by a Python rebuild (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}).
\begin{result}[\numeric\snap; \negres\ for $N\ge82$]\label{res:qmc}
The pair Hamiltonian is stoquastic (its only off-diagonal element is $-J$), so a path integral has no sign problem. A path-integral Monte Carlo with Trotter step $0.1$, single-spin, segment and world-line flips and replicas tempered in $\beta$ agrees with exact thermal diagonalisation at flow time $0.05$ and $\beta=25$ to $40$: the energy within $0.015$ and every $\langle\sigma^z_i\sigma^z_j\rangle$ within $0.02$ to $0.05$ at $10$, $12$ and $16$ pairs after $80\,000$ sweeps; without tempering a chain stays in one classical basin (errors up to $0.9$ at $10$ pairs). It does not converge at the sizes asked for. At $82$ pairs ($\beta=25$) two chains agree in the energy per pair ($-0.6976$ and $-0.6977$) but not in the correlations (rms difference $0.10$, maximum $0.41$); at $804$ pairs ($\beta=12.5$, $2400$ sweeps) their energies differ ($-0.7095$ and $-0.7036$, both still falling), the swap rate between replicas is $0.00$, and the correlations of the two chains are uncorrelated (rank correlation $0.07$). The landscape is the frustrated one of Result~\ref{res:ham-patch}; this does not bear on the bulk value of the unsatisfied weight.
\end{result}
\begin{result}[\numeric]\label{res:ham-flow}
Along the flow of Section~\ref{sec:flow}, on the clusters of $6$ to $14$ pairs, the largest pairwise concurrence peaks at $\qgYPeakLo$ to $\qgYPeakHi$ at $\tau=\qgYPeakTauLo$ to $\qgYPeakTauHi$, depending on the cluster, and vanishes at both ends. The largest CHSH value of any reduced two-pair state is $\qgYChshMax$, and $\qgYChshObs$ with the read-out visibility, above $2$ in one cluster of the five. At the Hat end the clusters are paramagnets with gap $4c$; the minimum of the gap moves with the cluster, so no transition is claimed.
\end{result}
\begin{result}[\numeric\snap]\label{res:ent-flow}
Write $\tau_{\mathrm{eff}}=J/(\kappa w)$, with $w$ the rms nearest-neighbour coupling. In an exact scan of $6$ to $16$ central pairs (level $3$, level $4$ for $16$) the largest pairwise concurrence peaks at $\tau_{\mathrm{eff}}$ of $0.4$ to $1.2$ for every size, below which the ground state is the cat of Result~\ref{res:ham-patch}; but it does not grow with size ($0.38$, $0.52$, $0.65$, $0.51$, $0.44$, $0.35$ at $6$, $8$, $10$, $12$, $14$, $16$ pairs) and the CHSH value above $2$ (up to $2.33$ at $10$ pairs) is gone at $16$. We read this as a finite-size effect with monogamy as the cluster grows, not as a phase or a persistent Bell-violating network. With the flow definitions ($h(\tau)=\tan(60^\circ(1-\tau))$, $J=e^{-h^2/2}$, $\kappa=\kappa_{\mathrm{norm}}h^2$ with the strongest coupling of level $2$ at the Spectre end equal to $1$) and the couplings recomputed at $b(\tau)$ (Result~\ref{res:flow-patch}), the flow starts at $\tau_{\mathrm{eff}}=0.244$ and crosses the window. For the ten central pairs of level $3$ the largest concurrence is $0.407$ at $\tau=0$ and $0.565$, $0.647$, $0.619$, $0.509$, $0.354$, $0.202$ at $\tau=0.02$, $0.05$, $0.08$, $0.13$, $0.2$, $0.3$, the best CHSH value $2.10$, $2.33$ (at $0.05$) and $2.03$ (at $0.3$), and the gap of the even sector $0.092$ to $1.958$; Recomputing the couplings from the exact vertices reproduces these numbers to the six printed digits. Rescaling the $b=1$ couplings by $(h/h_0)^2$ instead of recomputing them gives a coupling matrix off by $0.7\%$ at $\tau=0.05$ and $4\%$ at $0.3$, and a largest concurrence off by at most $0.014$. A parameter set outside the window, $J=1$, $\kappa=0.09$, $h=0.3$, sits at $\tau_{\mathrm{eff}}=190$ to $260$: a product state.
\end{result}

\subsection{A $\mathbb{Z}_2$ gauge form of the constraint}\label{sec:ham-gauge}
The patch has its own cell complex: tiles as sites, shared edges as links, interior vertices as plaquettes. Put a $\mathbb{Z}_2$ qubit on each link, a Gauss law $G_t=\prod_{l\ni t}X_l$ at each tile and a plaquette $B_p=\prod_{l\ni p}Z_l$ at each vertex.
\begin{result}[\exact]\label{res:ham-gauge}
At level $2$ the complex has $\qgYGTiles$ tiles, $\qgYGLinks$ links and $\qgYGPlaq$ plaquettes; it is a disc ($T-L+P=1$, also at levels $1$ and $3$). Every Gauss law commutes with every plaquette, their ranks over $\mathrm{GF}(2)$ are $T-1$ and $P$, and $T-1+P=L$: the plaquette limit has a unique ground state. The ledger is not the Gauss law: at every flat pair ($\qgYGPairsThree$ of $\qgYGPairsThree$ at level $3$) the shared vertex $P_8$ is a plaquette whose two links are the only links of the pair; $Z^m_{A_1}Z^m_{A_2}$ anticommutes with the two Gauss laws, its gauge-invariant forms are the Wilson lines along the two shared links, and their product is $B_{P_8}$.
\end{result}
\begin{result}[\exact]\label{res:flux-transfer}
The flux through a set $S$ of plaquettes, $\prod_{p\in S}B_p$, equals the product of $Z_l$ over the cut of $S$ (the links with exactly one end in $S$), checked on $100$ random regions at each of levels $2$ and $3$. Hence a link term $X_l$ changes the flux through $S$ exactly when $l$ is in its cut. The plaquette limit commutes with every flux, and the pair parity $-Z_{A_1}Z_{A_2}$, which commutes with every term of the tile Hamiltonian, is a flux conserved for the whole flow.
\end{result}
The tiling supplies the complex and the place of the ledger in it, not the Gauss law, which is a choice. In the plaquette limit the ground state has no two-body Bell violation.

\subsection{Contact graph, flux and the continuum picture}\label{sec:reconnect}
In the tetrad formulation of general relativity of Comisso and Asenjo \cite{comisso2026}, a vector potential $A^{\hat\alpha}$ has field $F^{\hat\alpha}=dA^{\hat\alpha}$; an ideal evolution conserves the flux of $F$ through transported surfaces and the connectivity of its field sheets, and a non-ideal term $R$ changes the flux at the rate $\oint_{\partial S}R$ (we did not check their derivations). Result~\ref{res:flux-transfer} is the discrete counterpart of this flux law: a link term $X_l$ changes the flux through $S$ exactly when $l$ lies in the cut of $S$. For the connectivity statement the two pictures part. In the lifted tiling the sheets are trivial (Result~\ref{res:lift-flux}), and on a two-dimensional complex the flux is pointlike, so connectivity of sheets has no $\mathbb{Z}_2$ analogue. What does change under a flip is the contact graph (Result~\ref{res:flip-contact}). Whether that graph is the discrete shadow of the sheets of a three-dimensional lift, and what would play the role of the non-absorbable term, is open; their theorems concern a smooth spacetime with a tetrad, and nothing here derives a statement of theirs on a tiling.

\section{The $14$th vertex: from the Spectre to the Hat}\label{sec:flow}
In the Spectre--Hat family a parameter $\tau\in[0,1]$ moves the tile from the Spectre ($\tau=0$) to the Hat ($\tau=1$): the length ratio $b(\tau)=a(1+(\sqrt3-1)\tau)$, the curve strength $k(\tau)=k_0(1-\tau)$ and the corner angle $\omega(\tau)=\omega_0(1-\tau)$ move linearly. The bit puts $P_8$ at $\pm h(\tau)$ from the chord, $h=a\tan(\omega/2)$; the distance between the two positions is the jump $d=2h$. At the Hat $\omega=0$ and the bit has merged (Figure~\ref{fig:flow3d}). The record, cost, validity and snap rules of the model are choices of the model, not results.

\begin{result}[\exact]\label{res:flow-area}
The area of the tile splits as $A_\pm=A_0(b)\pm ah$ with $A_0=2\sqrt3a^2+3ab+\sqrt3b^2$ independent of $k$ and $\omega$: the two bit values differ by $a$ times the jump $d=2h$ and by nothing else, and give the same tile iff $\omega=0$ (proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}). At $\omega_0=120^\circ$, $a=1$: $d(0)=\qgWJumpZero$, $d(1/2)=\qgWJumpHalf$.

\begin{result}[\exact\ and \numeric]\label{res:flow-validity}
The convex value $s=+1$ gives a simple closed curve at all $\qgWValidPoints$ points of a $5\times5\times11$ grid in $(b,k,\omega)$ up to $\omega=170^\circ$. The reflex value does not: for the straight tile ($k=0$, $a=1$) it is simple iff $h<h^*(b)=\min\bigl(\tfrac{3b+\sqrt3}{2},\tfrac{b+\sqrt3}{\sqrt3b-1}\bigr)$, so $\omega_c=2\arctan h^*$ is $\qgWOmegaCStraight^\circ$ at the Spectre and exactly $\qgWOmegaCHat^\circ$ at the Hat (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}); for curved edges (Figure~\ref{fig:critical}) at $\tau=0$ it fails for $\omega_0$ above $\qgWOmegaCThree^\circ$, $\qgWOmegaCFive^\circ$, $\qgWOmegaCEight^\circ$ at $k_0=0.3,0.5,0.8$.
\end{result}

\begin{result}[\exact]\label{res:flow-decouple}
In the level-$3$ patch the edges $7$ and $8$ of each of the $\qgWCoupledTiles$ tiles in flat pairs meet only the partner tile, so the opening of a pair cannot be seen outside it: any choice of states and of $\omega$ per pair closes every seam. What the whole patch shares is $b$ and $k$; only $\omega$ is free pair by pair.
\end{result}
\begin{result}[\exact\snap]\label{res:flow-patch}
Along the flow the tiling does not change: every vertex keeps its integer vector $(p,q,r,s)$ and moves to $x_b=(p+q\varpi)+b\,\zeta\,(r+s\varpi)$, which is the Tile$(1,b)$ geometry. In the patches of levels $2$ to $4$ the tiles of the minority orientation parity (Result~\ref{res:flip-parity}) are exactly the tiles with the edge lengths of Tile$(b,1)$ ($8$, $63$, $496$ of $71$, $559$, $4401$; $496/4401=0.1127017$ against $(5-\sqrt{15})/10=0.1127017$) and every other tile is Tile$(1,b)$, with areas $A(b,1)$ and $A(1,b)$ ($A(1,\sqrt3)=8\sqrt3$, $A(\sqrt3,1)=10\sqrt3$). Every flat pair consists of two Tile$(1,b)$ tiles, so the area ledger of a pair is $2A(1,b)$ at every $b$ and a bit moves area between its two tiles and nowhere else. The dipole couplings of the $14$ central pairs of level $3$ computed this way agree with an independent patch code to $10^{-14}$ at $b=1.2,1.5,\sqrt3$. The closed forms for the areas and the Mystic fraction are exact statements in $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt3)$ and $\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{15})$, checked here by the numerical agreement above.
\end{result}
\begin{result}[\exact\ for monotonicity, \negres\ for an arrow]\label{res:flow-arrow}
$A_0$, the reflex area $A_-$ and the jump $d$ are monotone along every admissible path in the box (up, up, down toward the Hat). The convex area and the bending energy are monotone along the linear flow but not along every path. Each of the three is a function of $b$ or $\omega$, so the orientation is a consistency check on the model, not a derivation of the direction of time. The allowed distinguishable bit values run $1,2,1$ for large $\omega_0$.
\end{result}
\begin{result}[\numeric\ and \negres]\label{res:flow-entropy}
The counting entropy of the bits of a patch ($\qgWPairsThree$ bits at level $3$ while a pair has two valid distinct open states, and $0$ otherwise) is non-increasing along the linear flow in $\qgWGridMono$ of $\qgWGridCells$ grid cells of $(\omega_0,k_0)$, exactly the $\qgWGridValidStart$ cells in which the reflex outline is simple at the Spectre end; elsewhere it rises from $0$ and then falls. It does not fall toward the Hat but at it. Read as an entropy it points opposite to ``later means more entropy'', and it is an orientation only for $\omega_0$ below $\omega_c(k_0)$.
\end{result}
\begin{result}[\numeric\ and \negres]\label{res:flow-sign}
The tangent angle of the outline lifts to a double cover in which it is antiperiodic (a spin structure that exists for every simple outline and needs no choice). The relative sign $-1$ between the two bit values would need a corner-turn difference of $360^\circ$ at $P_8$. Displacing $P_8$ by $\pm h(\cos\varphi,\sin\varphi)$ in any direction, for $b$ between $1$ and $\sqrt3$, $k_0\le0.3$ and all $h$ scanned, every deformation with two simple outlines has a difference below $2\pi$: the largest found is $\qgWDeltaSup^\circ$. So that sign is not reached by moving $P_8$; moving other vertices was not tried.
\end{result}
These results do not show that the bit at $P_8$ has anything to do with time. They show where the bit can be read, where it can be flipped and where a value is not allowed to exist.

\section{Relative entropy and the area ledger}\label{sec:rel}
Dorau and Much \cite{doraumuch} derive the semiclassical Einstein equations from the Araki--Uhlmann relative entropy $S(\rho\|\sigma)=\mathrm{Tr}\,\rho(\ln\rho-\ln\sigma)$ \cite{umegaki1962,araki1976}; the same programme on the Nariai horizon (source in Appendix~\ref{app:repro}) gives a relation in which $S_{\mathrm{rel}}=2\pi\,\delta Q$ is the boost energy of the excitation weighted by the modular flow and $\delta A=4G\,S_{\mathrm{rel}}$. Three ingredients are testable on the pair qubits: (i) whether the relative entropy of a region follows its boundary; (ii) whether an area observable of the model is proportional to it; (iii) whether the modular Hamiltonian $K_A=-\ln\rho_A$ of a region is a local energy, as it is for a wedge by the Bisognano--Wichmann theorem \cite{bisognano1975}. The reference state is the ground state (or a Gibbs state) of the flow Hamiltonian, entropies are in nats, and the relative entropy is computed from eigendecompositions of the reduced states and is infinite where the supports differ; every number below is copied from the logs of the code listed in Appendix~\ref{app:repro} ($\dagger$). The first law $S_{\mathrm{rel}}=\Delta\langle K_\sigma\rangle-\Delta S$, positivity, Pinsker's inequality and monotonicity under restriction are checked on random states; none of this uses the pair model. The ground states are those of the central pairs of level $3$ at flow time $0.05$ (Result~\ref{res:ent-flow}).

\begin{result}[\numeric\snap; \negres\ for an area law]\label{res:rel-volume}
For $8$ to $14$ pairs, the relative entropy $S(\rho_A(\tau+\delta)\|\rho_A(\tau))$ of compact and random regions $A$ for a flow step $\delta=0.004$ follows the number of pairs, not the boundary: the volume-only fit has rms $0.0003$ to $0.0011$ nats against $0.0007$ to $0.0015$ for the Delaunay boundary alone, and with both regressors the boundary coefficient is zero or slightly negative ($-0.0001$ to $-0.0003$ against $+0.0006$ to $+0.0013$ for the volume). This is the response of an extensive Hamiltonian to a uniform change of its couplings, so it says nothing about the area law of the entropy itself. It scales as $\delta^2$ (the ratio is $3.5$ to $4.5$ when $\delta$ is halved), while the change of the tile area of the region, $2m\,[A(1,b(\tau+\delta))-A(1,b(\tau))]$, is first order in $\delta$; for a region of three pairs $S_{\mathrm{rel}}/\delta A$ is $1.7\cdot10^{-2}$, $7.9\cdot10^{-3}$, $3.9\cdot10^{-3}$ at $\delta=0.008$, $0.004$, $0.002$: not a constant.
\end{result}
\begin{result}[\negres\snap]\label{res:rel-area}
Excite a region $A$ of the ground state of $8$ qubits by $e^{-i\alpha\sum_q c_qY_q}$, a unitary inside $A$, so that $S(\rho_A)$ is unchanged and $S_{\mathrm{rel}}=\Delta\langle K_A\rangle$ exactly (checked to $10^{-9}$). The model offers three area observables, read from the tiles (agreement $10^{-9}$): the area of the bump tile $A_0+hZ$ and of its partner $A_0-hZ$, which change by $\pm h\,\Delta\langle Z\rangle$, \emph{linearly} in the amplitude; their sum, the ledger of the pair, which does not change for any state; and the squared deviation $Q_A=(h\sum_{i\in A}Z_i)^2$, the only one quadratic in the amplitude. $S_{\mathrm{rel}}$ and $\Delta Q_A$ scale as $\alpha^2$ (ratio $4.0$ when $\alpha$ is halved) and the bump area as $\alpha$, so no linear area change is proportional to $S_{\mathrm{rel}}$. The ratio $S_{\mathrm{rel}}/\Delta Q_A$ is constant in $\alpha$ for a given region and generator, varies from $0.51$ to $0.98$ over four regions of $2$ to $4$ pairs, and over $30$ random generators on one three-pair region has mean $0.8$ and standard deviation $14.6$ (range $-50$ to $+49$): $\Delta Q_A$ has no sign while $S_{\mathrm{rel}}\ge0$, and $S_{\mathrm{rel}}/(\Delta A_{\mathrm{bump}})^2$ ranges from $2.7$ to $3500$. No constant of the type of $G$ relates an area observable of the model to the relative entropy.
\end{result}
The neutrality of the ledger is the structure of the zero-sum theorem of the Nariai-horizon derivation (Appendix~\ref{app:repro}), in which regions of negative flux integrate to zero against the modular weight; it is also the reason a neutral dipole cannot source net area. What is missing here is the other half of the Nariai relation, that $K_A$ is the energy:
\begin{result}[\exact\ and \numeric\snap]\label{res:rel-thermal}
Take a Gibbs reference $\sigma=e^{-\beta H}/Z$ of the flow Hamiltonian ($6$ qubits; $8$ qubits and a second region agree in shape) and the same local unitary. For the whole system $\Delta S=0$ and $S_{\mathrm{rel}}=\beta\,\Delta\langle H\rangle$ exactly (ratio $1.000000$ at $\beta=0.2$, $1$, $5$); $\Delta\langle H\rangle$ contains the Coulomb energy $\sum K_{pq}\Delta\langle Z_pZ_q\rangle$ of the area dipoles. For a region of three pairs, the fit $K_A=\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}H_A+\mathrm{const}$ of the modular Hamiltonian of the reference has $\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}=\beta$ and the relative residual and regional ratio $S_{\mathrm{rel}}(\rho_A\|\sigma_A)/\beta\,\Delta\langle H_A\rangle$ of Table~\ref{tab:thermal}: the local first law holds in the model when the state is hot ($\beta\lesssim0.5$, in units where $J=0.3$ and the strongest $K$ is $0.8$) and fails as it approaches the entangled ground state, where $\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}$ saturates at $3.7$.
\end{result}
\begin{result}[\numeric\snap; \negres\ for a rescue by negative temperature]\label{res:rel-negT}
The same quantities at $\beta<0$ (the Gibbs state $e^{-\beta H}$ with the sign of the temperature reversed; the spectrum of $H$ is bounded, so this is a mirror model) answer whether the local first law needs a positive temperature. The global law $S_{\mathrm{rel}}=\beta\,\Delta\langle H\rangle$ holds exactly for either sign (ratio $1.0000$ for $\beta$ from $-5$ to $5$). The residual of $K_A\approx\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}H_A$ is nearly the same for $\pm\beta$ ($0.008$, $0.034$, $0.084$ at $\beta=-0.05$, $-0.2$, $-0.5$, against $0.008$, $0.032$, $0.075$ at $+0.05$, $0.2$, $0.5$), $\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}$ follows $\beta$ in sign, and the regional ratio deviates from $1$ with opposite sign ($0.996$, $0.983$, $0.959$ at $\beta=-0.05$, $-0.2$, $-0.5$). At $|\beta|=20$ the residual is $0.35$ to $0.37$ for both signs. So the local first law is controlled by $|\beta|$: it holds when the state is hot, whether the heat is positive or negative, and a negative temperature does not extend it to the entangled regime.
\end{result}

\begin{result}[\numeric\snap]\label{res:rel-profile}
In the ground state $K_A$ is mostly one- and two-body and of the kinds of $H_A$: for four of $8$ pairs $X$ carries $37\%$ and $ZZ$ $53\%$ of the squared norm of its traceless part, no $Z$ or $Y$ at all; for five of $12$ pairs $20\%$ and $74\%$; but for five pairs of a different $10$-pair cluster only $54\%$ is one- or two-body, so the locality is not generic. The coefficient of $X_i$ over $-J$ is a local inverse temperature $\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}$, and the coefficient of $Z_pZ_q$ over $K_{pq}$ ($|K_{pq}|$ above a quarter of the largest, Delaunay-linked pairs) another, $\beta_{ZZ}$. For regions of $3$, $4$, $5$ pairs of $12$ and of $16$, $\beta_{ZZ}$ has mean $4.5$, $4.3$, $4.2$ and $4.5$, $4.5$, $4.2$ and $\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}$ has mean $3.3$, $3.6$, $4.2$ and $2.9$, $3.6$, $3.7$: one inverse temperature of about $4$ governs both terms. $\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}$ grows with the depth of the pair in the region (distance to the nearest pair outside): Spearman $0.63$, $0.74$, $0.80$ ($12$ pairs) and $0.56$, $0.79$, $0.74$ ($16$ pairs), for $\beta_{ZZ}$ $0.50$, $0.57$ and $0.43$, $0.38$ at regions of $4$ and $5$ pairs (null sd about $0.15$). The metric distance and the ledger depth (tile area of the region's pairs no deeper than the pair, $2A(1,b)$ each) give nearly the same correlation for $X$ ($0.67$ to $0.74$); the Delaunay hop count is clearly worse ($0.08$ to $0.38$). The profile fails at half the system: the mean $\beta_{\mathrm{loc}}$ for regions of $m=3,\dots,7$ of $12$ pairs is $3.3$, $3.6$, $4.2$, $-0.3$, $-2.1$ and for $m=3,\dots,10$ of $16$ pairs $2.9$, $3.6$, $3.7$, $2.1$, $-2.8$, $-3.6$, $-2.2$, $-0.8$, changing sign between $m/n=0.42$ and $0.50$ ($12$) and between $0.38$ and $0.44$ ($16$), with the correlation with depth reversed above half ($-0.77$ to $-0.82$ for $X$). The crossover is at a fixed fraction of the system, not a fixed size; for a region larger than half, $K_A$ has the spectrum of $K$ of its smaller complement.
\end{result}
These are one flow time, one cluster at each size and compact regions; whether the crossover fraction is constant as the system grows is open (two sizes). No area law is derived: the energy enters only through $\Delta\langle H_A\rangle$, and tying it to an area change would need $\kappa$ derived from the geometry, where it is a definition of the flow.

\section{The Hopf structure of the pair qubits and the tiers}\label{sec:hopf}
A pair qubit is a point of the Bloch sphere $S^2$ with a phase circle over it ($S^1\to S^3\to S^2$); two entangled qubits are a point of $S^7$ over $S^4$ by the quaternionic Hopf map (Appendix~\ref{app:repro} lists an implementation). This section asks what of that structure the model has, and whether the tiers of Section~\ref{sec:pairs} are linked by it. Numbers are copied from the logs of the code listed in Appendix~\ref{app:repro} ($\dagger$).

\begin{result}[\exact]\label{res:hopf-s4}
Write a normalised two-qubit state $(a,b,c,d)$ as the quaternion pair $q_1=a+bj$, $q_2=c+dj$. The map $h(q_1,q_2)=(2q_1\bar q_2,\ |q_1|^2-|q_2|^2)\in S^4$ has $2q_1\bar q_2=2(a\bar c+b\bar d)+2(bc-ad)\,j$. Hence its components $(x_1,x_2,x_5)$ are the Bloch vector $\vec r_1$ of qubit~$1$, and $(x_3,x_4)$ has length $C=2|ad-bc|$, the concurrence, so $|\vec r_1|^2+C^2=1$ (checked on random states to $10^{-12}$). The fibre, $(q_1,q_2)\mapsto(q_1u,q_2u)$ for a unit quaternion $u$, is a local $SU(2)$ acting on qubit~$2$ and does not move the point of $S^4$. The concurrence is not a function of $x_5$ alone.
\end{result}
For a pair of pair qubits in a larger state the reduced state is mixed and the map is not defined. The number $D=(1-|\vec r_p|^2)-C(\rho_{pq})^2\ge0$ measures the departure: it is the part of the linear entropy of qubit $p$ that is not pairwise entanglement with $q$, and it is $0$ exactly when $\rho_{pq}$ is pure.
\begin{result}[\numeric\snap]\label{res:hopf-leak}
In the flow ground state of the central pairs of level $3$ (each pair taken with its strongest-coupled partner, each pair counted once), $D=0$ for $2$ pairs at every flow time tried ($0$, $0.05$, $0.2$), so the $S^7\to S^4$ picture is exact there. For $8$ pairs the mean $D$ is $0.68$ at flow time $0.05$ and $0.06$ at $0.2$; for $12$ pairs it is $0.82$ and $0.11$. In the entangled window the entanglement of a pair qubit is shared with the rest of the system; the two-qubit Hopf picture applies on the hot side of the flow, where the entanglement is mostly pairwise.
\end{result}
\begin{result}[\numeric\snap; \negres\ for a derived tier link]\label{res:hopf-tiers}
The exact patches of different levels do not nest at one scale (the pair-cluster centroids of level $k$ are not a translated and rotated subset of those of level $k+1$; $4$ of $71$ tile centroids coincide after the best translation and rotation), so the model has no coupling between tiers of its own: a simple network model joins them by CX and CZ gates chosen for the purpose. Joining them instead by the dipole law of Section~\ref{sec:ham-pair}, with the central pairs of levels $2$, $3$, $4$ ($4$ pairs each) overlaid in one plane, is a model choice, and was done in two ways (cluster centres coinciding; or the level-$g$ cluster shrunk by $\lambda^{-(g-2)}$, $\lambda^2=4+\sqrt{15}$), with random offsets as a control. Results: (i) at flow time $\le0.13$ the ground state is the parity cat and every pair of tiers has mutual information $\ln2=0.693$; this is GHZ-type parity correlation, and with the doublet resolved into one classical pattern the mutual information is at most $0.0013$ nats at flow time $0.05$; (ii) at flow time $0.3$ the mutual information of tiers $(1,2)$, $(2,3)$, $(1,3)$ is $0.94$, $0.56$, $0.49$ nats for the centred overlay and $0.05$, $0.54$, $0.03$ for the scaled one, so the ordering ``the neighbouring tier is the most entangled'' depends on the overlay; (iii) an exact quench from $|0\dots0\rangle$ with no bridges gives at most $0.03$ nats up to $t=16$, against $0.21$, $0.42$, $0.26$ nats for the gates after three cycles, and an exact product without either; (iv) random shifts of the tiers entangle them in every sample, because the overlaid dipoles reach couplings up to $55$ times the intra-tier ones. The entanglement between tiers is thus a property of the overlay, not of the Spectre geometry.
\end{result}
What would make the link a result is a coupling derived from the substitution rule, in which a tile of level $k+1$ contains its level-$k$ children, so that the tiers are nested by construction; the code does not yet track parents and children. The \emph{Berry curvature} of the two-pair ground state over $(J,\text{chirality})$ is zero to $10^{-15}$ because the Hamiltonian is real, so a phase fibre over parameter space needs complex couplings that the model does not have.

\section{Scope and limits of the model}\label{sec:limits}
The results above fix what the model can and cannot say. Read as exact combinatorics and flip dynamics of an aperiodic tiling together with a quantum many-body model, it is complete in the sense that each claim is exact, enumerated or measured and labelled; read as a route to quantum gravity, it stops at the diagnostics below. It supplies a substrate with an exact finite pair structure (Section~\ref{sec:pairs}), a move set that is local only in a statistical sense (Section~\ref{sec:flips}: a flip is a cluster of about ten tiles whose density does not vanish with the step), and a Hamiltonian for its qubits (Section~\ref{sec:ham}). It does not supply an arrow of time. The flip relation is symmetric; every Hamiltonian of Section~\ref{sec:ham} is real, so a state evolved from an initial condition has $S(-t)=S(t)$ and its entropy grows away from that initial moment in both directions; and the orderings along the Spectre--Hat flow are functions of the parameters $b$, $k$ and $\omega$ rather than consequences of the dynamics (Results~\ref{res:flow-arrow} and~\ref{res:flow-entropy}). It does not supply a gravitational reconnection (Section~\ref{sec:reconnect}). It does not supply a relation between an area observable of the model and the relative entropy: the ledger of a pair is neutral and the bump area is linear in the amplitude where $S_{\mathrm{rel}}$ is quadratic (Result~\ref{res:rel-area}), and the modular Hamiltonian of a region is the energy only in the hot state, whichever the sign of the temperature (Results~\ref{res:rel-thermal} and~\ref{res:rel-negT}). It does not supply a coupling between tiers: the exact patches do not nest, and the entanglement of overlaid tiers is set by the overlay (Result~\ref{res:hopf-tiers}). The bit itself is confined to a window of validity: the reflex outline is a simple curve only below the critical angle $\omega_c$ (Result~\ref{res:flow-validity}), so the two positions of $P_8$ can be distinguished only for part of the flow, and they cannot be given the relative sign $-1$ by moving $P_8$ alone (Result~\ref{res:flow-sign}). Finally, since no closed flip cluster was reached, nothing is said about Pachner-type moves (Section~\ref{sec:flips-physics}).

\section{Open problems}\label{sec:open}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{Do flip clusters tile?} Refill the holes of the flips that are valid tilings with the exact search of Section~\ref{sec:flips-cavity}, and reproduce or refute the reported $2$-tile swap at $k=4$ with reflections (this needs the erased tiles of that report). A closed cluster also needs a placement list of radius about $26$, after which the change $(dV,dE,dF)$ of each cluster can be compared with Pachner moves.
\item \textbf{A flip rate that depends on the level.} Cluster size and density against the step at the tight tolerance, for the other eleven classes and level $4$; a Langevin simulation against $T^{\alpha/2}$. A level-dependent rate is the only candidate for an arrow from flips.
\item \textbf{The pair rule.} Prove from the seam automaton that the $53$-type table of Result~\ref{res:ham-rg-closed} has no new type at any level.
\item \textbf{The dipole model.} A converged or exact solution for $N\ge82$ pairs, to decide whether the frustration reaches a bulk value near $0.29$, and clusters beyond $14$ pairs along the flow. Tempered path-integral Monte Carlo does not converge at $82$ and $804$ (Result~\ref{res:qmc}); a method that handles the glassy landscape (many more replicas, or a cluster update for mixed signs) is needed.
\item \textbf{The modular Hamiltonian at larger size.} Whether the crossover fraction of Result~\ref{res:rel-profile} stays near $0.4$ to $0.5$ beyond $16$ pairs, whether above half the weights of $K_A$ are those of the complement seen from the other side, and whether a thermal reference of the flow at an intermediate temperature carries the Nariai relation $S_{\mathrm{rel}}=\beta\,\Delta E$ into the entangled regime.
\item \textbf{Nested tiers.} Track which tiles of level $k+1$ are the children of a tile of level $k$, derive the coupling between tiers from that nesting instead of from an overlay, and ask whether the iteration-to-iteration entanglement of the proposal that the universe stops at a finite $N$ arises from it (Result~\ref{res:hopf-tiers}); also the $S^7\to S^4$ structure of Result~\ref{res:hopf-s4} for the pairs that stay pure beyond $2$ pairs.
\item \textbf{Connectivity and the bit.} A notion of connectivity on the tiling for which a flip is a reconnection, and a deformation moving vertices other than $P_8$ that reaches the relative sign $-1$, or a proof that none does.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}
The paper's positive results are on the tiling and on the qubits: exact pair counts and placement rules, the structure of flips, and a pair-qubit model with a frustrated classical ground state and an entanglement window. Its quantum-gravity diagnostics are mostly negative or undecided, as set out below. A phason flip of the Spectre is a cluster of tiles at uniform density, symmetric under reversal, that in most cases rewires the contact graph of the tiles around a footprint while leaving the tetrad flux of the lift at zero. The pair structure carrying the model's qubits is exactly finite: pair counts obey a proved recurrence, their placement rule is a $53$-type table that closes at level $4$, a hole of up to $300$ tiles has one refilling, and the dipolar pair model has a unique frustrated classical ground state up to $28$ pairs. The model has no arrow of time of its own and no demonstrated link to Pachner moves; what remains is narrow and testable, namely whether the flip clusters are tilings, whether a closed cluster exists, and whether the flip rate depends on the level. The pair qubits entangle in a window the Spectre--Hat flow crosses; the relative entropy of their regions follows volume, no area observable is proportional to it, and the modular Hamiltonian of a region is a local energy only when the state is hot, with a depth-graded local temperature in the ground state up to about $40\%$ of the system. The Hopf map $S^7\to S^4$ of two pair qubits is exactly the Bloch vector of one qubit with the concurrence as the length of the remaining two coordinates, and it describes the model only where the entanglement is pairwise; the tiers are not linked by anything but a chosen overlay.

\section{Proofs}\label{app:proofs}
\emph{Proof of Result~\ref{res:flow-area}.} Let each of the $14$ B\'ezier edges be point symmetric about the midpoint of its chord ($P_0+P_3=P_1+P_2$). The reflection through the midpoint of the chord is a rotation by $\pi$; it maps the edge to itself reversed and the chord to itself reversed, so the loop (edge, then chord back) goes to the same loop with reversed orientation. A rotation preserves signed area and reversal changes its sign, so each edge encloses signed area $0$ with its chord, and the area of the tile is that of its chord polygon, $A_0(a,b)=2\sqrt3a^2+3ab+\sqrt3b^2$, for every $k$. The flat vertex $P_8$ is the midpoint of the straight chord $P_7P_9$ of length $2a$; moving it by $\pm h$ along the normal adds or removes the triangle $P_7P_8'P_9$ of base $2a$ and height $h$, by the shoelace formula, so $A_\pm=A_0\pm ah$. At the end of an edge the tangent is the reflection of the one at its start, hence parallel to it, and a graph over the chord keeps its tangent in a half plane, so the net turning of each edge is $0$; by the Umlaufsatz the corners of a simple outline carry the whole turning $\pm2\pi$. Moving $P_8$ by $\pm h$ turns the chords of edges $7$ and $8$ by $\varphi=\arctan(h/a)=\omega/2$ and changes the corner turns at $P_7,P_8,P_9$ by $(-\varphi,2\varphi,-\varphi)$ and $(+\varphi,-2\varphi,+\varphi)$ for the two values, a second difference. \qed

\emph{Proof of the straight-tile part of Result~\ref{res:flow-validity}.} With $P_8=(\tfrac{3b}2+\sqrt3,-\tfrac{\sqrt3b}2)$, the edge $P_{12}P_{13}$ is the segment $x=\tfrac{\sqrt3}2$, $-\tfrac32\le y\le-\tfrac12$, which the horizontal path of $P_8$ meets at $h=\tfrac{3b}2+\tfrac{\sqrt3}2$. The vertex $P_{11}=(b+\tfrac{\sqrt3}2,-\tfrac32)$ lies on the side from $P_9$ to $P_8'$ when $h(\sqrt3b-1)/2=\tfrac b2+\tfrac{\sqrt3}2$. The two thresholds are equal at $b_\times=(1+2\sqrt7)/(3\sqrt3)=\qgWBx$. That no other vertex or edge is met earlier was checked on $25$ values of $b$ against a direct test of simplicity, so the statement is exact for the two contacts and checked numerically for the rest. \qed
